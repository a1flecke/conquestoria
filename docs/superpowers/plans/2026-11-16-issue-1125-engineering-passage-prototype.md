## Authored world and scenarios

Use a small fixed map with a surveyed isthmus, two Western/Eastern home ports and two Southern supplier endpoints. Set procurement caps, source inventory and a real consuming use of the purchased stock. Measure both the travel estimate and the executed round trip. Target eight rounds via the lawful detour versus four to six via the passage. The fixture must produce two accepted loaded returns on each corridor with overlapping evidence by a common checkpoint, under the actual load, turnaround and settlement cadence. Assert the budgets, supplier capacity, at least one actual stock-enabled action, and a continued need for repeat service after first qualification. If the routes fail this setup, adjust the authored geography, inventory or demand before testing. Keep the evaluator thresholds fixed for that review round, and record actual timing. Include a matched replay with the passage disabled.

Reuse the same fixture and change only the named inputs for each replay:

| Replay | Setup and expected proof |
| --- | --- |
| Independent | One actor controls the passage and both home imports. A funded expedition already has assignments and earmarked funds. Use one national capital authorization for either the passage or a port supply/defense commitment whose benefit is established before choosing. Approving either leaves the original expedition operating. The passage creates a useful leg and stock before it can report a qualification. An expedition expansion above the reserve limit requires a separate informed authorization. |
| Shared | Give Eastern home port J and its serving hull to the partner. Agree on an open working connection and one substantive freight role before either delivers that role. Maintain one counted corridor each, with the commissioning party supplying access. Participants earn equal engineering standing after both roles are fulfilled; a token supplier, a late reimbursement or a participant with an unfulfilled role gains no winner standing. Switching hot-seat player exposes only the current viewer's details. |
| Operating setback and recovery | Seed a visible competing opportunity at setup. Suspend actual operation using a revoked prospective permission or a real closure that respects existing terms. Delivery history remains inspectable while qualification fails. Restore operation through a lawful capable replacement; a material modernization needs a fresh qualified settlement per affected corridor. Existing rights and evidence survive a rival's independent project. |
| Regional near miss | Let both useful services run while their endpoints span only the same two authored macroregions. Show the profitable/valuable shortcut and the missing geographic reach. Include a closure case with sufficient historical deliveries, an unused open passage, and an eliminated claimant with otherwise eligible service. Each breaks one necessary condition. |
| Second geography and availability | Move the bottleneck so only one major route substantially improves, or bring a known viable alternate detour closer. Keep the competing priority valuable. Check that deferral is a sensible choice here even for a player who committed in the first map. After the authored probe, sample ordinary seeds, including a map with no viable connection, to estimate how often discovery exposes a credible opportunity. |

Start with 80 liquid gold after committed expenses and a 30-gold protected minimum. Set competing capital quotes at 45 each, leaving five uncommitted gold after either decision. Fully fund the existing expedition separately. Before the choice, establish from the scenario receipts when the next 45-gold commitment becomes affordable. Give the alternative a real payoff before that point: for example, a funded defensive ship commissioned in time to escort a known vulnerable ordinary route. On the passage map, let earlier supplies unlock a real existing use in time to matter. On the second geography, reduce the actual route benefit while preserving the alternative's cost and payoff. If a second purchase is routinely available before either benefit or if the alternate only exists in copy, reject the fixture as a test of tradeoffs. Players may still defer both.

Use a fixed schedule for opponent decisions during the first replays, backed by represented actions and visible evidence. This tests understandable counterplay. Open-ended AI planning and general difficulty are later work. For an early AI parity check, script a decision through the same action contract, including one denial case without secret intel.

### Misleading UI risks

Show “available to commit” only for a known feasible option with legal terms, available spending and a viewer-permitted preview. Missing undisclosed permissions or unsurveyed geography must not be inferred from a badge or forecast. “Operating” requires working service. “Useful delivery” requires a real settled stock claim. The rival's summary must remain unchanged when only the owner's hidden counter, shipments or expedition plans change. Test invalid offers, expected versus settled goods, and local services that lack regional reach.

### Interaction replay checklist

Inspect, dismiss, reopen, authorize and repeat-click during and after acceptance. Check changed costs and access while Confirm is open, failed stale commands, permission loss during a loaded return, and hot-seat switching with a private notice and confirmation open. Check focus return, keyboard activation, touch targets at a narrow mobile viewport, and a tap on the panel never selecting the map underneath. Put the status and primary action in text, with icon and map line style as secondary cues. Use existing queues and ETAs if they satisfy the design; a change to queue behavior requires a rendered order and ETA replay for add, reorder and remove. Duplicate notices and stale or unreachable actions fail the test even when the underlying state is correct.

## Evidence and stop decisions

1. Establish a failing positive passage or settlement trace and targeted negative cases before modifying source. Start with matching movement, route runner and settlement tests in `tests/systems/`, then `tests/core/turn-manager.test.ts` and the existing science-victory tests for outcome ordering. Run the source rule check on changed source, all mirrored focused tests and the verification impact requirements for the actual diff.
2. Assert legal route/path agreement, actual home stock, unique receipt, conjunctive qualification, stable same-round results under actor-order changes, accepted shared role versus token aid, stale action denial, save/reload determinism and the visible map and panel updates. Cover a hidden-state-only change for a rival viewer and repeat with hot-seat transition. Run an interaction through the live player surface and inspect rendered state, not just the simulation model.
3. Run an uncoached first-time replay on desktop and touch. Observe each dimension below. Ask the player to explain what changed, why they chose their commitment, and where they would look for the next action. Record exact interactions and empty turns, then compare the second geography and the matched disabled-passage run.
4. Continue when the operating benefit is real, the regional near miss is clear, and geography or competing commitments can rationally flip a choice. Simplify or revise using observed evidence before asking for production victory rules. Keep release thresholds, full AI and balance for later rounds.

| Dimension | What to observe | Redesign signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Excitement | The player anticipates an opening or shipment because it affects something they already want. A visible rival decision or access change creates a real response. | Their first meaningful event is the result popup, or the scenario needs a forced disruption to hold attention. |
| Gameplay | The player can explain what each of two affordable commitments would change and what choosing either defers. In the matched run the early delivery or benefit differs. | One move dominates both maps or the route effect exists solely in a counter. |
| Fun | The player wants the shortcut for its ordinary use and can point to meaningful actions between commitment and payoff. Count repetitive interactions and forced waiting. | They move ships manually to feed proof or advance several uneventful turns purely for the result. |
| Lovability | The player remembers the named passage, a place it helped and why they built it. Leave a readable history of first useful service and any earned joint credit. | They recall a threshold or generic award and cannot say what their civilization accomplished. |
| Replayability | Geography, rights and real demand change the appeal of building, deferring, or teaming up. Watch a replay choice and ask for the reason before revealing the result. | Only the quantities change, every map needs a canal, or shared play amounts to splitting the bill. |
| Playability | A new player can find the passage from the advisor or Victory Progress, understand the cost and next action, and distinguish forecast from delivered impact on both input modes. | A coach must explain live status, a failure loses the path back, or the UI leaks hidden facts. |

For determinism, replay the final authored delivery one round before and on a canonical finish, with two independent qualifiers in an evaluator fixture and reordered candidates. Verify that a genuinely eliminated claimant is excluded and canonical hot-seat handling and victory events stay intact. An engineering-only result authorizes no terminal transition. The written result uses the completed round and is recorded once per undertaking, with later accomplishments separate. A save from outside the opt-in scenario should have no active evidence or new UI.
