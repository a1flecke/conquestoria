## Authored world and scenarios

Start from a small fixed navigable map with a surveyed isthmus. Give home port H in Western and home port J in Eastern a purchased-stock need, and use a Southern supplier coast with two legally reachable ports. H–Southern and J–Southern each take an eight-round legal no-passage trip and a four- or six-round best trip via the passage as fixture targets. Choose hex distances, hull allowance and service approach against the actual movement runner. Build assertions on computed trips and observed delivery, and adjust the authored hexes if the engine contradicts the targets. Preauthorize cap and reserve limits and use seller inventory, ship caps and procurement quantities that require two loaded returns on each successful corridor. Consume the stock through a real represented use. Run long enough for four real settlements in the 12-round evidence window. Adopt the first actual settlement timings as fixture data once measured.

Reuse the same fixture and change only the named inputs for each replay:

| Replay | Setup and expected proof |
| --- | --- |
| Independent | One actor controls the passage and both home imports. A funded expedition already has assignments and earmarked funds. Use one national capital authorization for either the passage or a port supply/defense commitment whose benefit is established before choosing. Approving either leaves the original expedition operating. The passage creates a useful leg and stock before it can report a qualification. An expedition expansion above the reserve limit requires a separate informed authorization. |
| Shared | Give Eastern home port J and its serving hull to the partner. Agree on an open working connection and one substantive freight role before either delivers that role. Maintain one counted corridor each, with the commissioning party supplying access. Participants earn equal engineering standing after both roles are fulfilled; a token supplier, a late reimbursement or a participant with an unfulfilled role gains no winner standing. Switching hot-seat player exposes only the current viewer's details. |
| Operating setback and recovery | Seed a visible competing opportunity at setup. Suspend actual operation using a revoked prospective permission or a real closure that respects existing terms. Delivery history remains inspectable while qualification fails. Restore operation through a lawful capable replacement; a material modernization needs a fresh qualified settlement per affected corridor. Existing rights and evidence survive a rival's independent project. |
| Regional near miss | Let both useful services run while their endpoints span only the same two authored macroregions. Show the profitable/valuable shortcut and the missing geographic reach. Include a closure case with sufficient historical deliveries, an unused open passage, and an eliminated claimant with otherwise eligible service. Each breaks one necessary condition. |
| Second geography and availability | Move the bottleneck so only one major route substantially improves, or bring a known viable alternate detour closer. Keep the competing priority valuable. Check that deferral is a sensible choice here even for a player who committed in the first map. After the authored probe, sample ordinary seeds, including a map with no viable connection, to estimate how often discovery exposes a credible opportunity. |

Use one explicitly shared national authorization in the first fixture. Start with 80 liquid gold after already-committed expenses and a 30-gold protected minimum, with the expedition's existing assignments fully funded separately. Set competing capital quotes at 45 each. That allows either commitment immediately, with five uncommitted gold afterward. Authorize and account for the new passage's maintenance within its own budget. Establish from the fixture's real treasury and turn flow when the other commitment becomes affordable again. The alternate priority should produce an observable supply or defense result before that point. On the second geography, keep its cost and payoff constant while the passage loses meaningful reach. These values define the choice probe only. A player may still defer both projects.

Use a fixed schedule for opponent decisions during the first replays, backed by represented actions and visible evidence. This tests understandable counterplay. Open-ended AI planning and general difficulty are later work. For an early AI parity check, script a decision through the same action contract, including one denial case without secret intel.

### Misleading UI risks

Show “available to commit” only for a known feasible option with legal terms, available spending and a viewer-permitted preview. Missing undisclosed permissions or unsurveyed geography must not be inferred from a badge or forecast. “Operating” requires working service. “Useful delivery” requires a real settled stock claim. The rival's summary must remain unchanged when only the owner's hidden counter, shipments or expedition plans change. Test invalid offers, expected versus settled goods, and local services that lack regional reach.

### Interaction replay checklist

Inspect, dismiss, reopen, authorize, repeat-click during and after acceptance, and reopen after the state update. Repeat for shared offer selection, withdrawal of an unaccepted offer, denial after changed rights, and viewer switch with a private panel open. Confirm canonical approval occurs once, current labels rerender immediately and permitted alternatives remain reachable. The design adds no dedicated construction or route queue. Use existing queue controls and ETAs only if the prototype reuses them; any change to their behavior requires rendered order and ETA coverage for add, reorder and remove.

## Evidence and stop decisions

1. Establish a failing positive passage or settlement trace and targeted negative cases before modifying source. Start with matching movement, route runner and settlement tests in `tests/systems/`, then `tests/core/turn-manager.test.ts` and the existing science-victory tests for outcome ordering. Run the source rule check on changed source, all mirrored focused tests and the verification impact requirements for the actual diff.
2. Assert legal route/path agreement, actual home stock, unique receipt, conjunctive qualification, stable same-round results under actor-order changes, accepted shared role versus token aid, stale action denial, save/reload determinism and the visible map and panel updates. Cover a hidden-state-only change for a rival viewer and repeat with hot-seat transition. Run an interaction through the live player surface and inspect rendered state, not just the simulation model.
3. Ask a first-time player to explain the before/after route, the payoff they observed, the remaining eligibility condition and what their commitment displaced. Capture where they click, wait or need help. Compare two geographies and the reasonable alternative. Record one reason to retain, simplify or reject the loop with the specific observed evidence.
4. Continue if the operating benefit is real, the regional near miss is legible, and geography or commitments can flip the best choice. Simplify if routine play creates repeated city/ship chores or if players mainly read a checklist. Revise if the result depends on a fake settlement, weak rival pressure, a dominant commitment or an opaque threshold. Defer generic thresholds, release-level ending changes, full AI and scenario-balance work until this evidence exists.
