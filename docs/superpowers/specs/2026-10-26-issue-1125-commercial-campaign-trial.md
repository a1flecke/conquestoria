# Commercial Legacy: comparative campaign budget and setback trial

Advisory investigation for issue #1125, published as a planning snapshot on 26 October 2026 at the user's request. Recommendations below remain for review; publication is not acceptance of them. No implementation. This is a constructed economic scheduling experiment with invented inputs, not a game-engine, AI, generated-world or human playtest. It compares consequences under declared assumptions without asserting calibrated balance or enjoyment.

## Findings and recommendation

Retain the opportunity/power distinction, their combinations and the 30/20 versus 40/20 trial thresholds. Under the tested inputs, the preferred plan changes with available cash and real existing preparation. A mixed plan can finish sooner, but a pure opportunity or pure power plan can be the best available plan in another starting position. No universal conclusion about relative difficulty follows.

The severe setback exposes the important failure: one operating relationship can be so consequential that losing it leaves a nominal recovery path too slow to contest the ending. Explain actual known exposure and feasible responses before commitment. Do not call every mathematically possible recovery a useful comeback, impose guaranteed substitute partners or add repeated repair chores to balance power.

Do not add more attribution machinery from this trial. The next experience requirement is that these commitments produce real useful capabilities and consequential choices on the map. Abstract payments followed by larger numbers are insufficient, even when their arithmetic balances.

## Common starting position

- Applicable world productive reference: 100, including the contender's 10 domestic activity already under substantial exercised authority. No qualifying opportunity extent is established initially.
- Starting treasury: 60. Net operating receipts: 8 per turn, after existing upkeep and obligations. Other already funded civilization activities are outside this incremental comparison.
- National development capacity: two work units per turn. Each listed work requirement represents stipulated actual development/integration, not a compulsory victory timer or generic fee for gaining credit.
- All seven options are available to both strategies. The world supplies the necessary actual skills, counterpart permissions, inputs and demands. This is a strong analytical premise, not evidence that generated worlds or rational opponents will always provide these offers.
- Listed costs cover incremental provision in the fixture; listed income is net incremental receipts after its ongoing costs. Ordinary operation is automated. Each application works only on completion; receipts start on the following turn. Completion can contribute extent at the ordinary checkpoint.
- New productive activity increases the world reference. Each new application is initially under actual contender authority as well as linked to its substantial opportunity contribution. Organizing an existing application changes exercised authority without adding new output in this deliberately controlled case.
- Domestic and foreign output sets are disjoint between applications. New Kestrel capacity and pre-existing Kestrel capacity are distinct activity. Repeated intermediate transactions do not create additional extent.

The actual substantial achievement, meaningful operating variety, attribution, survival and continuing functions are stipulated for completed qualifying applications in this calculation. Purchasing work alone cannot establish those predicates in the real design. These are analyst-known values, not authority to reveal hidden world totals or rival states.

## Method and limits

For each permitted option set, enumerate all orders of its applications. In an order, complete one application before starting the next, using up to two affordable work units each turn. If cash cannot support two, spend only on affordable work; no debt or unfunded construction. Finish at the first checkpoint meeting either complete numerical extent test. Compare earliest finish, then remaining cash at that same finish.

This is an exhaustive comparison within the declared serial-plan model, not global optimization over all possible parallel, interrupted, hired, redirected or diplomatically renegotiated plans. The three option sets are new applications only, existing-authority applications only, and all applications. They are analytical restrictions, not exclusive player classes or declarations required by the game.

## Baseline outcome

| Opportunity applications only | 16 | 34 | 212 | Domestic, Senna new, Kestrel new, Daru new |
| Existing-authority applications only | 16 | 6 | 236 | Kestrel authority, Senna authority, Daru authority |
| All options | 15 | 16 | 224 | Domestic, Kestrel authority, Senna new, Daru new |

The first produces O=43, Of=33, R=143 and qualifies for opportunity extent. Its P=53 is below the 40% power total requirement. The second has P=40, Pf=30, R=100 and qualifies for power extent.

The mixed result has O=31, Of=21, P=53, Pf=33 and R=131. Opportunity is incomplete, but power is 40.46% overall and 25.19% foreign, so the power extent qualifies. It is not a blended partial-claim victory. The new 31 is already part of the 53 under authority; do not add it again.

The mixed schedule first completes domestic production on turn 4, with cash 44 and net receipts increasing from 8 to 11. Kestrel authority completes on turn 8, leaving 16 and increasing net receipts to 14. Senna new industry completes on turn 12, leaving 16 and raising net receipts to 16. Daru new activity completes on turn 15, leaving 16. This is an affordable persistent plan, not a demand that the player issue work orders every turn.

The authority-only plan has a work-capacity lower bound of 15 turns but needs 16 under the baseline cash constraint. Its minimum treasury is zero, which is permitted because only affordable actual work is performed and ongoing obligations are included in receipts. No extra reserve floor is imposed.

A flexible choice set necessarily contains the pure plans; flexibility being no worse is not itself a discovery. The useful finding is that the selected plan is not always mixed or always one pure approach. These reversals demonstrate conditional alternatives in the authored fixture. They do not establish how frequently each situation occurs or prove that the trial thresholds are fair.

## Setback: Kestrel takes over operation

At the end of turn 6, after that turn's ordinary receipts and work but before the victory checkpoint, a legitimate change in Kestrel's operating arrangements ends the contender's authority and incremental operating fees there. The physical economic activity continues. Its new productive capacity, when delivered, can still support opportunity attribution. Already accepted development access remains valid in this case. No destruction, reduction in world reference, automatic diplomatic punishment or retroactive breach disqualification is introduced.

This is a stipulated external event, not a universal right to cancel binding contracts. An actual campaign must establish how such a transition can occur under real terms, competition or other permitted actions. The event affects both existing and new Kestrel operating authority and fees; it does not erase historical creation. No salvage value, movable replacement fleet or additional contractor is assumed available by this fixture.

Take each baseline plan's actual state after turn 6, including cash spent and incomplete work. Only then replan using all remaining options. There is no clairvoyant change to turns 1–6. Previously invested work persists, but abandoning a partially developed option does not refund it.

| Initial plan | Position after the setback | Earliest tested recovery after replanning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opportunity-focused | Domestic application complete; Senna new half complete; cash 38 | Opportunity on turn 16; cash 33 |
| Authority-focused | Kestrel authority completed but now lost; Senna authority partly complete; cash 14 | Opportunity on turn 24; cash 1 |
| Mixed | Domestic application complete; now-unhelpful Kestrel authority half complete; cash 30 | Opportunity on turn 18; cash 19 |

In the mixed recovery, stop further Kestrel-authority work and complete Senna new, Daru new and Kestrel new. The lost four work units and 36 cash remain real costs. The new Kestrel application still contributes to opportunity; it no longer supplies authority or operating fees in this branch.

If the original authority-only plan simply continues, it completes its three original applications on turn 19 and still fails extent: P=28 and Pf=18. Its tested recovery instead leaves the partial Senna-authority work unfinished and builds the four new applications. Total spending reaches 312, including sunk work. This is a costly change of strategy, not a reset or a free comeback.

## A rival approaching victory

Suppose a rival independently fulfills all its requirements on the common turn-18 checkpoint. This is a conditional deadline comparison, not a simulated rival budget or permission to reveal an exact hidden deadline.

- All three baseline finishes precede it.
- After the setback, opportunity-building still finishes first on turn 16.
- The mixed recovery reaches turn 18 and can be an independent simultaneous winner under the accepted ending rule if every requirement genuinely holds.
- The authority-focused recovery at turn 24 loses this race. Do not describe it as a viable comeback merely because eventual qualification remains mathematically possible.

The result does not justify forcing a new customer to accept terms or guaranteeing a replacement asset. It shows why commercial concentration and the affordability of actual alternatives must be understandable before a major commitment. Additional real assets or services could improve recovery; their existence must be demonstrated, not invented after a bad result.

## Gameplay and experience verdict

**Strategic variety: conditional pass.** Cash, existing preparation and prices change the selected plan. Opportunity can remain relevant when authority is replaced. Mixed play can finish through one full achievement without an exclusive track choice.

**Recovery: mixed result.** The mixed plan has a plausible two-turn diversion in this fixture. The concentrated authority plan suffers a severe setback and can be out of the race. That may be a legitimate competitive defeat when exposure was understandable; it is poor onboarding if sold as equally resilient or if ordinary worlds force every power contender into this trap without meaningful choice.

**Fun and playability: not established by the schedule.** The numerical model compresses every application into work, expense, income and scope. Implemented literally, it could become a queue of purchases and waiting. Actual provision must change supplies, usable production, access or other concrete capabilities; actual authority must create consequential operating decisions. Stable operation should remain automatic. No requirement to change allocations merely to prove activity.

The player should choose whether to establish new capacity, accept a consequential operating arrangement, retain a profitable use of equipment or redirect it when genuinely available. The same evidence used for victory must explain the real consequence. It cannot be a decorative story attached to a progress purchase.

**Lovability: candidate opportunity.** Kestrel's development can remain part of the player's history even after Kestrel operates independently. The mixed player can finish by completing a productive transformation instead of reimposing dependence. That is a coherent story, not an observed emotional response.

## Guidance from other games

Offworld Trading Company's official gameplay description makes supply, demand, scarce claims and the choice to buy or produce consequential. Borrow context-dependent choices; do not import stock buyout as the commercial finale. The fixture's sensitivity reversals are the sort of strategic behavior we want to test, not evidence that we reproduce Offworld's experience. [Official gameplay](https://www.offworldgame.com/game/gameplay).

Factorio's developers explain that reducing small logistical hassles creates room to enjoy additional systems. Apply that lesson to stable plans and changed circumstances: automate routine execution and expose meaningful bottlenecks, without replacing strategic decisions with repeated work allocation. [Developer discussion](https://www.factorio.com/blog/post/fff-374).

## Proposed product conclusion and verification

Keep both approaches and combinations; retain the trial thresholds for now. Require truthful known exposure and actionable, actually available alternatives in plan previews. Do not imply that an eventual mathematical recovery can beat a rival. Do not add a commercial rescue entitlement or a finance/attribution subsystem to compensate for an overly fragile example.

Treat useful world consequences as a prerequisite for candidate acceptance. If a candidate is only a budget, a wait and a larger scope number, revise its actual economic interaction before implementation. Do not solve boredom by adding compulsory crises or more notifications.

Twenty-four selected schedules were independently replayed across the six starting-condition comparisons and three setback continuations/replans. Checks covered work capacity, affordability, construction progress, next-turn receipts, actual scope and cash reconciliation. The serial-order search does not establish a global optimum over all legal gameplay actions. The numeric input costs, rates, authority effects, recipient willingness and deadline are authored assumptions. No statistical balance, generated-world coverage, live UI comprehension or player enjoyment has been measured.
