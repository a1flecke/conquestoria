# Water-engineering Legacy: campaign and decision checkpoint

7 November 2026. Advisory backup for [issue #1125](https://github.com/a1flecke/conquestoria/issues/1125), read with the [portfolio](2026-10-26-issue-1125-legacy-portfolio.md) and [exploration checkpoint](2026-11-07-issue-1125-exploration-campaign.md). Accepted directions are product candidates. All campaign outcomes, prices, percentages and physical assumptions below are paper-test constructions, not implemented gameplay, validated hydrology, calibrated economics or empirical proof of enjoyment.

## Accepted experience and qualification

Water engineering changes where substantial civilization can thrive. A profitable terminal, famous dam, cheap power, many pipes or several successful local services is insufficient. Existing technology can enable extraordinary engineering; no compulsory scientific breakthrough or era floor is implied.

Count an affected population when works overcome a major constraint and people substantially use the resulting capability. Dependable farming where water prevented cultivation, usable land and settlements, essential water supply, or materially new transport access can qualify. Slightly cheaper irrigation on already dependable farms, faster loading at an adequate port, occasional deliveries and unused advertised capacity do not establish transformation.

The constraint and attainable consequence should be understandable before investment. Actual use matters, but no compulsory population-growth tail is imposed on mature settlements that already substantially benefit. Farming, households and businesses can develop through ordinary civilization systems; the player does not place or manage every beneficiary.

Useful transformation appears on the map and in normal play: viable settlement sites, dependable agricultural districts, connected markets, roads and transport connections usable by settlers, traders and armies. Profit supports the work; depth and breadth establish its Legacy. Serving a customer does not automatically qualify that customer's whole population. Beneficiaries of overlapping projects count once.

Successful infrastructure can become ordinary without losing significance. It does not need repeated disasters or perpetual novelty to count. Conversely, "lasting" does not mean indestructible, immune to conquest or owned forever regardless of sovereignty. Capture transfers ownership; foreign operation requires consent and operation may require knowledge/capability. Historical authorship, current functioning and winner eligibility remain distinct.

## Finite operating loop and player styles

Discover an opportunity, prove the approach, develop at substantial scale, enjoy lasting capabilities and choose the next frontier. A solved region becomes dependable civilization infrastructure. Do not invent recurring faults merely to keep its owner busy.

Routine bookings, renewal within approved terms, operating releases and budgeted maintenance are automatic and inspectable. Surface new major investments, materially restrictive commitments, access disputes, significant capacity changes and strategic threats. Automation follows an authorized spending budget/reserve floor; it must not silently exhaust the treasury by recruiting crews.

Entrepreneurial, public-investment and cooperative approaches are all candidates. The first uses services and retained control to finance works; the second uses national wealth directly; the third shares costs/access/responsibilities. Players can mix them. None requires copying the user's business empire. Shared winner eligibility still requires substantial qualifying contribution and advance clarity; customers or lenders do not automatically win.

The user's standing style: independence, retained ownership/control, recruit parallel crews when supported, seek additional sources, monetize plausible resources/technology/land/services, and prefer recurring income. Fixed acquisition or infrastructure exchanged for permanent rights is preferred over annual easement payments. In the wetlands negotiations, the accepted financing preference was four years of stated fixed payments then ownership, with operating control from the outset. This is a negotiated campaign choice, not a universal contract duration.

Temporary operating deficits are acceptable while treasury reserves support them. Every component need not be independently profitable; a commercially strong civilization may finance a larger transformation. Existing obligations, maintenance and full purchase price must remain in the affordability calculation.

## Campaign progression and lessons

### Home riverlands: one region, not many victories

Upper Fork channel work, the replacement bridge, Southbank storage, miller supply, storm shelter, farm basins and the later canal all belonged to one home region. Distinct project names and distant customers did not create extra geographic breadth.

The user negotiated bridge tolls, auctioned deliverable water rights, bought safer land and planted a replacement orchard before abandoning the old productive site, reinforced seepage-prone works, and combined fishing, concessions and storage with useful provision. Relocation is possible and can cause costs and dissatisfaction. A replacement orchard is not instantly mature.

The delta trials separated port protection, freshwater farm supply and pilot/escort access. A storm barrier did not magically supply freshwater. Existing fisher access and prior water rights mattered. A paid early escort agreement was not retroactively repriced because a storm increased demand.

The canal employed geology, hydrology, civil engineering/planning and business investigation together. Alternatives differed in relocation, land, leakage and water access. Repeated loaded operation exposed a limited initial source; storage, water-saving operation, alternate feeders and staging berths addressed different constraints. Dry-season tours, repairs and accepted excavation materials were possible income, not guaranteed substitutes for passage demand.

A short upstream river-port extension added a warehouse, berths and a small crew inn only where investigation supported use. The user judged the resulting achievement more commercial than a world water victory. Major canal construction finance was never reconciled; narration advanced to test gameplay, not to establish affordability.

### Inland plateau: diverse supply and actual use

Workshop settlements, farms and a caravan settlement had different shortages. Early and late crop trials combined agronomy with restaurants that developed demand; introductory seed discounts and small purchase commitments were bounded by real demand. Processing/preservation and actual repeat buyers mattered. No unspecified miracle crop or universal disease benefit was established.

The user added scouts for snowpack-fed sources and an aqueduct. Permanent diversion/corridor rights were negotiated in exchange for a functioning local cistern/feeder transferred to the mountain community, with its existing use protected. The main network remained controlled within the agreement.

A poor-snow investigation sought independent backup. A western source remained a candidate while southern groundwater trials affected household supply and recovered too slowly; that option was allowed to fail. In the constructed low-snow test, established commitments continued but unsupported extra acreage was not opened. Restaurants and tourism were commercial support, not automatic extra engineering footprint.

### Southern wetlands: solve the physical problem, then finance it

The user rejected a narrow choice between raised roads and protected villages, requesting geologists, hydrologists, civil engineers, mosquito specialists and business investigation. Reconnecting blocked channels, managing storage and releases, improving household containers and restoring access were considered together. Observed breeding/nuisance reduction was not proof of reduced clinical disease.

The constructed watershed comprised three headwater settlements, six central settlements and two low-basin settlements. Coordinated releases mattered: improving one place must not simply transfer its flood peak to another. Seasonal water sales, collection services and concessions could support operations. Storage also needed safe overflow and drainage; newly created shallow ruts could themselves create problems.

The user required faster returns than an initial six-to-eight-year prospect. A central acquisition trial used total capital 24, four annual payments of 6, and a target annual operating surplus of 8 before payments. An illustrative first year earned only 7 before payments because debris cost an extra 1. This exposed recurring debris costs rather than proving a dependable margin.

Future contracts included normal debris handling; exceptional removal used agreed cost allocation, with recovered-material receipts credited and operator-caused costs retained by the operator. No retroactive charge was added to an existing agreement.

For a later flood disruption, the user's instruction was "today and tomorrow": pay the immediate transfer cost, then investigate an owned, revenue-producing place to route future excess water. Candidate storage separated a retained fish/tourism pool, saleable water and empty flood reserve. Those capacities cannot all be sold as the same available space. Insufficient head ruled out a proposed mill in the paper example.

A reservoir expansion trial proposed total capital 16 paid as four annual 4 installments, incremental recurring revenue 8 and operating cost 3, leaving 1 after purchase payments. This excluded already-owned land and excluded speculative tourism upside from base income. It remained incremental to central obligations; it was not a whole-campaign solvency proof.

The final wetland redesign considered upstream flood storage because an enlarged low outlet alone could fail during high river conditions. Seasonal land uses could earn money while preserving flood space; unrestricted permanent development there would defeat the design. Ordinary-year benefits included dependable roads, productive land and meaningful settlement development. A major flood illustrated the system working under standing policy, not a required gate-opening minigame or random victory prerequisite.

### Eastern coast: foothold, independent supply, then real scale

Survey the coastal watershed and salinity/storage/access constraints while completing the wetland design. Extending plateau supply could sacrifice its drought reserve; a backup cannot be promised simultaneously to both regions. Local seasonal capture and a modest paid port terminal were the preferred investigation direction, contingent on physical feasibility and affordability.

The terminal could fund further exploration before distant farms could be served. The user retained existing crew assignments and sought another crew for promising independent supply. A limited, time-bounded port reservation with minimum payments preserved future sources/infrastructure and operating control. This was preferred to selling most future capacity to one customer. A lucrative commitment can legitimately delay the water Legacy while advancing commerce; it is not automatically a mistake or credit toward every path.

The later coastal completion was explicitly a hypothetical fast-forward assuming viable supply and funding. It was not an achieved, budget-validated campaign state. A functioning terminal earns commerce, local usable water earns local transformation, and a proposed extension earns no accomplishment yet. Broad farming/settlement/transport use, not opening an empty reservoir, establishes the larger transformation.

## Commercial policies learned in the paper campaign

These preserve the user's expressed strategy; figures are scenario tariffs, not global defaults.

- Owned farm basins: offer early bookings at a lower price, increase prices through the season, restart the schedule annually, and increase the opening rate by 1 every three completed years. Reinvest a trial 50% of net profit into useful deepening, widening, intake or leak improvements until marginal demand no longer supports expansion. Auction water/service rights, not an unintended sale of the basin or operating concession.
- Sell proven deliverable water after existing commitments and required reserves; a larger hole does not create supply. Distinguish stock, replenishment, seasonal timing and peak service capacity. Do not count the same clean water or flood space twice.
- Canal passes: nonrefundable seasonal bundles of 5/10/15 one-way crossings; a round trip consumes two. Early example prices were 18/35/51 versus a single crossing at 4, then midseason 23/45/66 versus 5 and late 28/55/81 versus 6. The annual opening schedule increased by 1 per crossing every three years. Existing confirmed prices were protected.
- Passes were fleet-bound, nonresale and expiring; operator failure earned agreed credits. Timely release restored a credit, no-shows consumed one. Scarce-departure premiums were separate from base passage. Auction known peak demand early; deliberately withholding capacity for hypothetical late buyers could lose revenue. Do not overbook.
- Small-business facilities followed demand: warehouse for waiting goods, repair/waiting berths, crew lodging or tourism where supported. Avoid constructing every ancillary business automatically.
- Excess-flow/pressure riders used published normal thresholds, observable triggers, preagreed services/rates and beneficiary allocation. Normal seasonal operation remained included. Credit prepaid allowances and recovered materials; no retroactive billing or fee for operator-caused failure. Premium capacity and cheaper base-plus-overage offers were alternatives. Do not manipulate water management to create fees.

## World-scale calibration: current trial, not final balance

All home projects were grouped into one region. The six-region world is a schematic calibration, not a generated map. Equal drawn region boxes did not imply equal population, land or opportunity. The earlier local visualization showed only the home transformation and should not be treated as the current campaign record.

| Region | Share of world population | Illustrative substantially transformed share of world population |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Home riverlands | 24% | 18% |
| Inland plateau | 18% | 12% |
| Southern wetlands | 20% | 9% |
| Northern lakes | 14% | 0% |
| Eastern coast | 16% | 0% |
| Ocean islands | 8% | 0% |
| Total | 100% | 39% |

Candidate wetland completion adds 8 percentage points, reaching 47%; candidate coastal transformation adds 10 to the existing network, reaching 49%; both reach 57%. These are assumed qualifying footprints, not estimates derived from verified settlement data. Do not count everyone near an asset as transformed to make the table come true.

The accepted trial is at least 50% of world population substantially benefiting, together with depth, actual function and meaningful environmental breadth. It supersedes the earlier water-specific 20%/three-applications/5%-each trial for this population-focused scenario. It is not a universal requirement for engineering or ecological legacies. Population alone could undervalue sparsely populated transformations; alternate measures remain unresolved.

Supporting areas must be substantial relative to the strongest area; token satellites cannot rescue one enormous home region. The user previously accepted the 8+4+4 half-main-region relationship for Ocean, and the water discussion retained its breadth principle. Defining comparable water regions/impact and the exact distribution rule remains unfinished. Do not silently treat Ocean's marine-area formula as a finalized water formula or impose a fixed checklist of environments.

Ordinary capacity or customer reach covering 57% fails if it does not overcome major constraints. Conversely, established beneficial growth can legitimately expand the affected population. Overlap or moving the same citizens between serviced sites must not multiply credit. War-driven depopulation must not instantly make an unchanged network win by shrinking the denominator; the accepted devastation/new-normal direction needs explicit timing calibration, especially across eras/speeds.

An independent empire may qualify within controlled territory if it actually meets world scale and breadth. It bears those costs and cannot rely on guaranteed foreign acquiescence. The exact extra difficulty for isolated play and availability across different maps remain unproven. Customers abroad are not a mandatory substitute for actual significance.

The wetland-versus-coast capital comparison was also a target, not a signed budget: wetlands 48 total / 12 annual purchase payment / 16 operating surplus before payment; coast 60 / 15 / 19, each leaving 4 during four-year purchase terms. Existing debts are additional. No reconciled global treasury, capital plan or full funding schedule exists. Do not present the desirable margins as demonstrated feasibility.

## Finale, disruption and information

Proof accumulates through actual operation and development. A final commissioning can culminate the achievement only if the established evidence completes the requirements; an untested opening does not create proof. No extra ceremony timer, required rare disaster or obligation to wait for new settlement growth when mature populations already qualify. Resolve eligible simultaneous finishers at the shared normal checkpoint.

Rival counterplay affects the real capability underlying the achievement:

| Action | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Undercut port prices | Reduce finance and possibly slow expansion; do not erase still-functioning farms |
| Sabotage a pump while reserves/alternate supply maintain service | Repair expense and reduced resilience; current transformation remains functional |
| Disrupt expansion while existing commitments remain served | Buy time without deleting the established achievement |
| Cause essential regional capability to actually fail | Affected qualification can be lost; no blanket Legacy immunity |
| Block access needed for an unfinished district | Delay that development; alternate routes, security, diplomacy or another project can respond |

Do not add a new arbitrary outage timer in this checkpoint; detailed interruption/recovery handling must remain consistent with earlier shared Legacy rules. A damaged component is not synonymous with a failed region. Redundancy is useful because of actual continuity, not protected progress points. A strategically useful new route can also become an invasion route worth defending. Do not script a mandatory attack whenever an investment succeeds.

UI should explain the actionable gap, e.g. "Coastal expansion interrupted. Existing settlements remain supplied." Routine work is summarized; meaningful commitments and threats interrupt. Victory warnings must respect fog and legitimate intelligence, without exposing private sources, reserves, equipment or customers. The paper population percentages do not authorize an omniscient player-facing census. Exact notification and evidence contracts remain to be designed.

These references motivate design questions. No comparative reception study or playable prototype demonstrated fun, excitement or lovability in this checkpoint.

## Stopping point and next bounded validation

Stop adding routine water-contract scenarios. The local loop has enough examples to expose the main operating and attribution problems. The candidate remains provisional, not implementation-ready.

1. Test whether the 50% finish and meaningful distributed depth feel world-changing, especially whether the 57% example feels like four ordinary utilities. Strengthen qualifying transformation before demanding repetitive extra construction.
2. Reconcile the whole-campaign economy: treasury, overlapping capital installments, crews, infrastructure, maintenance, failed monetization and plausible counterpart demand. Test self-funding and public/cooperative approaches without conveniently inventing buyers.
3. Test different map shapes, populations, environments and rival personalities for viable routes and repeatability. Distinguish legitimate unavailable paths from accidental forced cooperation or one optimal recipe.

Detailed measurement, observation/privacy, AI incentives, interruption rules and numerical tuning belong to those tests. Preserve the larger request for several distinct victory experiences and multiple player personalities; do not let this water campaign expand into a standalone economic simulator.
